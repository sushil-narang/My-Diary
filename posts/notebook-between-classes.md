A short one today. Between lectures I keep a paper notebook — the kind that
smells of stationery shops — and flipping through it, I noticed most entries
aren't about AI at all.

They're about people learning.

## The notebook entries

*"Rohit stayed back to ask a 'stupid' question. It wasn't. Half the room had
the same one."*

I see this every term. The question nobody asks is almost always the one
everybody needed. I've started asking it myself, deliberately, on behalf of
the room.

*"Fatima's face when her model finally ran — first time in three weeks she
looked up."*

Some weeks a student is underwater in every class. The fix is almost never
more theory. It's one working result, however small.

*"A final-year student came back to say she uses the evaluation checklist
from my class in her job. Nobody told me they kept any of it."*

We hand out frameworks all semester and never see which ones survive contact
with real work. When one does, it's worth writing down.

*"Today I explained the same concept four different ways. The fourth worked.
Why is it always the fourth?"*

Because the first three are how *I* understand it. Teaching is mostly the
search for the fifth way — the one that fits the person in front of you.

## Why I keep the notebook

It's easy, in a technical field, to believe the value is all in the content —
the models, the maths, the code. The notebook reminds me otherwise.

The content is the easy part to transfer. Confidence, curiosity, the willingness
to ask the question — those travel differently. Slower. Person to person.

That's the part of the job I'd keep even if the syllabus didn't require it.
