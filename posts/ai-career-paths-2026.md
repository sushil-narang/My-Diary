"Sir, what jobs are actually *there* for us after this degree?"

I get some version of this question every semester, usually in week eleven,
usually from students who've just seen a frightening headline about AI replacing
jobs. So I drew a map. This is roughly what's on my whiteboard.

## The roles that actually hire

**1. The builder**
Machine learning engineer, applied AI engineer, MLOps engineer.
Writing models, putting them into products, keeping them running in
production. The largest bucket by far.

*What it needs:* solid programming, one framework properly, and the ability to
debug systems rather than notebooks.

**2. The specialist in a domain**
AI in healthcare, finance, agriculture, manufacturing, education.
People who understand *both* the field and the models. Rarer and better paid
than pure builders.

*What it needs:* willingness to learn a second domain properly. Our students
who take a minor outside CS consistently stand out here.

**3. The translator**
Applied data scientist, AI product analyst, solutions engineer.
Sitting between business people and technical teams, turning vague asks into
well-posed problems — and results back into decisions.

*What it needs:* communication more than cleverness. The ability to say "that
question can't be answered with this data" and be believed.

**4. The researcher**
M.Tech/PhD, labs, and increasingly industry research teams.
For students who genuinely enjoy six months with one hard question.

*What it needs:* patience and mathematics. Not the same thing as being the
top coder in the batch — several strong researchers were average programmers.

**5. The teacher**
Educators, curriculum designers, content creators, trainers.
The path I took. Corporate AI training in particular has grown sharply as
companies upskill entire workforces.

*What it needs:* the ability to make something difficult feel possible.

## The part nobody mentions

Three truths I wish someone had told me:

1. **The first job is not the career.** Almost nobody I know works in exactly
   the area they started in. Depth first, breadth later.
2. **AI is a feature, not always a job title.** A lot of our graduates do
   "normal" software or data roles — with AI skills that make them the person
   on the team who gets asked.
3. **The hype cycle is not the job market.** Headlines move fast; hiring moves
   in steps. What stays valuable across cycles: fundamentals, clear writing,
   and shipped projects.

## What I tell them to do this semester

- **One project you can defend.** Depth beats a gallery of tutorials.
- **Explain it in writing.** A clear blog post about your project is a
  surprisingly effective interview answer.
- **Learn to evaluate.** Anyone can run a model now; knowing whether it's any
  good is the scarce skill.
- **Talk to three people doing the job.** An afternoon of conversations beats
  months of guessing.

The honest answer to "what jobs are there?" is that there are many, they move
quickly, and the students who do a few things thoroughly have never had
trouble finding them.
